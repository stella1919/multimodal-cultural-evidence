# Project Summary

This project develops a reproducible workflow for turning museum images, structured metadata, dates, geography, and cultural descriptors into traceable multimodal cultural evidence. It asks what computable visual and semantic similarities appear across cultural contexts, whether visual similarity aligns with or decouples from cultural semantics, and whether temporal, geographic, and contextual integration reveals potential cross-regional associations and patterns of cultural evolution.

The pilot uses real open-access records from The Metropolitan Museum of Art Collection API. It combines API caching and validation, transparent context categorization, OpenCLIP visual embeddings, multilingual sentence-transformer text embeddings, cosine similarity, dimensionality reduction, exploratory clustering, similarity networks, and century-level temporal analysis. Structured metadata textualization is explicitly distinguished from original museum narrative descriptions. Outputs are framed as computational observations and candidate cases for qualitative interpretation; they do not prove historical transmission or causal influence.

The project extends my interests in cultural heritage and multimodal human-centered research toward large-scale computational cultural analysis and AI for the humanities.
